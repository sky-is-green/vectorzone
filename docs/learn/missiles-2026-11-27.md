# Missile corrections & point defense — 2026-11-27

## Reproduced root causes

1. **Radar arrow/position mismatch** — contact projection and heading rotation
   used opposite sign conventions. A missile at world +X was placed on the
   right of the scope while its arrow pointed left. The same inconsistency
   affected tanks and the bomber.
2. **Early disappearance** — a missile spawned at z = 205 with a target at
   z = -190 expired at age 1201 around z = -75, over 110 m short. The hard
   20 s lifetime and tank-only collision below 3 m were both insufficient.
3. **Horizon invisibility** — missiles used the green, fog-affected aircraft
   line material with no solid or screen-facing cue. The wireframe collapsed
   to a tiny point when viewed nose-on.

## Corrections

- One camera-aligned radar projection and `radarDirectionYaw()` now drive all
  positions and heading markers. Positive world yaw still displays left.
- Missiles aim initially at the target's real 3-D position, use a distance-based
  20–50 s lifetime, a 280 m safety boundary, and swept 3-D collision.
- Missiles now have a dedicated fog-free orange line material, solid core,
  exhaust trail, and camera-facing vector diamond.
- Target selection preserves the 55% player-lock chance. The remaining weighted
  pool favors supertanks and the bomber over light tanks.
- Bomber and supertank point defense emit `defenseFire` bursts and guarantee an
  intercept before impact. These are visual events, not shells: no radar reveal,
  no cooldown interaction, no self-damage.

## Verification

- 154 tests: radar agreement, distance lifetime, 3-D collision, target weights,
  bomber/super intercepts, no defense at range, no radar reveal, determinism.
- Browser: radar ahead/arrow agreement at hull heading π/2; horizon probe center
  lit pixels 34 → 293 with the missile present; bomber defense showed tracer
  bursts, intercepted the missile, and survived. Zero console errors.
- Full gate: 156.5 KB gzipped.

Human feel remains open as F12: arrow direction while turning, horizon
readability in real play, far-side approach reliability, and point-defense
spectacle.
